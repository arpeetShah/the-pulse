McKinney Has a Startup Scene and Most Locals Have No Idea

By The Pulse Founder  ·  5 min read

📍

McKinney gets talked about a lot — fastest-growing city in the country for a stretch, the downtown square, the new developments going up every few months on the northern edge of town. What doesn't get talked about nearly as much is the quiet layer of entrepreneurship running underneath all of it. There are people building things here, and most of the city has no idea.

McKinney has been ranked among the top cities in Texas for small business growth for three of the last five years, according to data from the Texas Economic Development Corporation. The city's population growth — it's gone from about 54,000 in 2000 to over 215,000 today — has created demand that local entrepreneurs have been quick to meet. The businesses that tend to thrive here aren't the ones transplanted from somewhere else. They're the ones built specifically for this community.

"What makes McKinney interesting isn't that it's a startup hub in the Silicon Valley sense. It's that the growth has created real market gaps, and the people who live here have been filling them faster than big companies can notice."

The teen founder angle is the part I find most interesting. There are high schoolers in McKinney running actual businesses — lawn care operations with multiple clients, social media management for local small businesses, tutoring services that operate entirely through word of mouth. Most of them don't call themselves entrepreneurs. They just needed money and found a gap. But that's what entrepreneurship is, stripped of the TED Talk vocabulary.

The downtown square has become something of an incubator in practice, if not in name. The turnover of retail spaces there is low compared to comparable suburban downtowns in North Texas, which means the businesses that open there tend to stay. The Saturday farmers market draws a consistent crowd that local food and craft businesses have learned to depend on as a testing ground before going broader.

If you're a high schooler in McKinney and you've thought about starting something, there are more resources available than you probably know. The McKinney Chamber of Commerce runs a young entrepreneurs program. The Collin County Small Business Development Center offers free consulting. And the customer base — a city of 215,000 people with above-average household income and a strong local identity — is genuinely supportive of businesses that feel like they belong here.

The startup scene in McKinney doesn't look like the ones you see in movies. There's no co-working space with kombucha on tap. There are just people, quietly building things, in a city that keeps growing around them. That's actually the better version of the story.
