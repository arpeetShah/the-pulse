I'm a high school freshman from McKinney, Texas. I started The Pulse because I couldn't find anything written for people like me — not at us, not down to us, but actually for us.

Why I Started This

Most media aimed at teenagers either talks down to them or tries too hard to be relatable. I wanted something different — something that actually respects how smart high schoolers are, and gives them information they can use.

Every week I pick one topic that matters to real high school life. I spend time actually researching it — reading studies, looking at data, talking to people — and then I write about it the way I'd explain it to a friend. No fluff, no filler, no condescension.

The Pulse launched in 2025. It goes out every Sunday morning. It's free. It always will be.

What The Pulse Is

The Pulse is a weekly publication covering the topics that actually matter to high schoolers — sports and performance, money and entrepreneurship, mental health and lifestyle, science and how things work, and local stories worth knowing. It is written by a high school student, for high school students, backed by real research and cited sources. It is the one place where being young doesn't mean being talked down to.

What We Cover

Sports

Athletic performance, training science, recruitment, and the mental side of competing at the high school level.

Money

How to earn, save, and think about money as a teenager — before anyone else teaches you these things.

Mental Health

Stress, sleep, identity, pressure — the real stuff nobody talks about enough, grounded in research, not just vibes.

Science

The fascinating, weird, and genuinely useful science behind everyday things — written so it actually makes sense.

Local Spotlights

Stories from McKinney and the broader North Texas area — the people, businesses, and moments worth knowing about.

How I Write

Every article starts with something I've personally experienced or noticed — a question I couldn't answer, a tension I kept running into, something that didn't add up. Then I actually go research it. I read studies. I check sources. I look for the thing that's true, not just the thing that sounds good.

Then I write it peer to peer. Not like a textbook. Not like a news wire. Like one high schooler explaining something real to another. Short paragraphs. Plain language. No jargon unless I explain it. Every claim that matters has a source at the bottom of the page.

That's the whole model. Real topics, real research, real voice.
